I am a postdoctoral fellow at the [Center for Theoretical Physics – a Leinweber Institute](https://ctp.lns.mit.edu/) at MIT, where I work with [Mikhail Ivanov](https://physics.mit.edu/faculty/mikhail-ivanov/), and a Junior Investigator at the [NSF AI Institute for Artificial Intelligence and Fundamental Interactions](https://iaifi.org/). Before coming to MIT I was a Cosmology Fellow at [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://www.lbl.gov/) and the [Berkeley Center for Cosmological Physics](https://bccp.berkeley.edu/) at UC Berkeley, working with [Simone Ferraro](https://sferraro.lbl.gov/) and [Nathalie Palanque-Delabrouille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathalie_Palanque-Delabrouille). I did my PhD at the [University of Cambridge](https://www.kicc.cam.ac.uk/) with [George Efstathiou](https://people.ast.cam.ac.uk/~gpe/), after studying physics at [ETH Zurich](https://ethz.ch/en.html) and writing my master's thesis at Stanford with [Leonardo Senatore](https://www.phys.ethz.ch/the-department/people/person-detail.MjgyMzgw.TGlzdC84MzgsMTE3MjU5OTI5OQ==.html).

**Cosmology.** I'm a data-driven cosmologist working at the intersection of theory, computation, and observation to uncover the origin, composition, and ultimate fate of the Universe. My work bridges cosmology and astrophysics by developing theoretical frameworks and advanced statistical techniques that extract maximal information from cosmological data. Recently, I've focused on the Lyman-α forest as a probe of the large-scale structure, using it to test models of inflation, explore the nature of dark matter and dark energy, and constrain neutrino properties. I build new statistical tools and machine learning methods designed to harness the full potential of current and upcoming surveys, while also integrating this information with measurements of the Cosmic Microwave Background—the Universe's earliest observable light—to map its evolution over cosmic time. While the standard cosmological model has been remarkably successful, I see observational cosmology as a gateway to new physics and deeper insights into the workings of the cosmos.

**Physics and AI.** I am equally interested in how artificial intelligence and science can advance each other. AI can sharpen the traditional steps of research, such as simulation, modelling and statistical inference, but it can also open new ways of doing science: generating and testing models, scaling inference to problems that were out of reach, and probing how far automated scientific reasoning can go. Physics is a good place to develop such methods: it offers a well-defined sandbox spanning theory, computation and experiment, and what works there often carries over to the other sciences. I also care about the wider questions this raises: how new technology changes what research is possible, how rapidly improving AI is reshaping scientific practice, and how academic and industry research relate to one another. In 2026 this work was recognised with an Anthropic AI for Science award.

I am always happy to hear from students and potential collaborators.
